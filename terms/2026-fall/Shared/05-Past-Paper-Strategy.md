# Past Paper Strategy

## When to Attempt Past Papers

| Phase | Timing | Papers | Condition |
|-------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Familiarization | Week 3 | Browse 1 paper (no solving) | 30 min |
| Timed midterm | Week 7 | 2 papers (full timed) | 2 hr each |
| Post-midterm analysis | Week 9 | Review midterm paper | Identify weak areas |
| Distributed | Weeks 10-12 | 1 paper per week (partial topics) | 1 hr each |
| Full finals prep | Week 14 | 2+ papers (full timed, 3 hr) | Closed book |

## How to Solve a Past Paper

### Step 1: Scan (2 min)
- Count numerical questions vs theory
- Identify which diagrams are required
- Spot the "gimme" questions (answer first)
- Spot the "trap" questions (answer last)

### Step 2: Order of Attack
```
Numericals first (35-40% of marks)
  → Diagrams (15-20% of marks)
    → Theory (remaining marks)
```

### Step 3: Timed Execution

| Question Type | Time Budget | If Stuck |
|--------------|-------------|----------|
| Numerical (subnet, CRC, CPI, Bayes) | 5-8 min each | Flag, move on, return |
| Diagram (datapath, header, DFD) | 8-12 min each | Start outline, fill details |
| Trace (A*, pipeline, GA) | 10-15 min each | Write every step clearly |
| Theory (3-5 marks) | 3-5 min each | Use 5-point structure |
| Theory (10+ marks) | 10-15 min each | Outline first, then flesh |

### Step 4: Self-Grading
- Grade immediately after finishing (not next day)
- Be honest about partial marks
- Log every error in Master Error Log with:
  - What you wrote
  - Correct answer
  - Why you got it wrong
  - What to drill

## After Each Past Paper

1. Calculate topic-wise accuracy
2. Identify bottom 3 topics
3. Add extra retrieval sessions for those topics
4. Re-test within 1 week
